# ADR-0019 — HNSW keeps the layer-0 graph connected by construction, and repairs it

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-08-11

## Context

At the full 38,211-vector corpus this implementation's HNSW graph fragmented
into **5,400+ layer-0 components**, the largest holding ~62.5% of nodes.
Nodes in disconnected components are unreachable from the entry point by any
search, filtered or not, so recall was capped by construction: ~0.88
unfiltered (vs hnswlib's 1.000 on the same data) and ~0.484 filtered at 1.91%
selectivity (vs IVF-Flat's 0.949). The `hnswlib` reference showed no such
ceiling at full scale, so the data was exonerated and the defect was this
implementation's construction (ADR-0010, ADR-0012).

The mechanism was measured, not guessed. Two failure modes:

1. **Birth isolation.** When a fresh insert overflowed a neighbour's edge
   list, the diversity heuristic re-run from the neighbour's point of view
   could evict the newly inserted node; the symmetric stale-edge removal then
   stripped *all* of its forward edges, leaving it unreachable.
2. **Eviction to degree 0.** An *existing* node whose last surviving edge was
   to an overflowing neighbour lost that edge to the same stale-edge removal
   and ended with degree 0 — invisible to every future search.

## Decision

Three changes to `_insert` / `build`, in ascending order of defence:

1. **Insert-time protection.** The node being inserted survives every prune it
   participates in: when a neighbour overflows, the pruned list keeps the
   insert and evicts its least-useful member instead.
2. **Orphan protection.** When the symmetric stale-edge removal would strip an
   existing node's last edge (it has degree 1 and only points at the
   neighbour), swap it back into the pruned list in place of a member that has
   another edge to lean on — a node can never be evicted to degree 0.
3. **`_repair_connectivity`.** A deterministic post-build pass: union-find the
   layer-0 edges, then bridge each residual component to the **largest**
   component at the closest node pair, in one chunked matmul. Bridging to the
   largest component (not the entry point) spreads the extra edges across
   distinct nodes instead of turning one node into a super-hub that every
   search must expand.

## Alternatives

- **Bridging every component to the entry point** — reached 100% connectivity
  but turned the entry node into a 3,555-edge hub; every search expands it.
  Rejected in favour of closest-pair bridges to the largest component (entry
  degree 30).
- **Only repairing, no orphan protection** — leaves the repair pass to bridge
  3,525 components instead of 231; more surgery per build and a graph that
  keeps re-fragmenting. Protection at the source is cheaper than repair.

## Consequences

**Positive.** Re-measured on the full corpus: **0 isolated nodes, 1 component
(100% of nodes), entry-point degree 30, 230 repair bridges.** Unfiltered
tie-aware recall 0.951 @ ef=64 → **0.983 @ ef=256** (was 0.885 @ ef=256).
Filtered pushdown at 1.91% selectivity **0.948 @ ef=64 → 0.995 @ ef=256**
(was 0.484) — HNSW now matches IVF-Flat (0.949) instead of lagging it, and
meets the NFR-3 ≥ 0.95 bar under the temporal filter. All 231 tests pass.

**Negative.** The repair pass adds a bounded build-time cost: union-find plus
one distance matmul against the largest component per small component (230
components at this scale; ~seconds). Orphan protection can, in rare cases,
keep a node at budget+1 edges when no member with a spare edge exists to swap
out — bounded, and far cheaper than a disconnected graph. The synthetic
selectivity sweep was measured pre-fix; the Day-5 benchmark regenerates it
once.
