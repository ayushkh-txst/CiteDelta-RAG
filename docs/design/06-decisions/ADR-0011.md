# ADR-0011 — The temporal predicate is pushed into the index

**Status:** Accepted · **Date:** 2026-08-11

## Context

A versioned corpus stores every version of a rule, and only one is in force at
a time. On this corpus the temporal selectivity is ~1.9% (724 of 37,911 chunks
admissible at `as_of = 2019-06-01`). Filtering an *approximate* result set after
the fact cannot be trusted: the k nearest neighbours can all be out of force.

## Decision

Both retrievers enforce admissibility *inside* the search:

- `LexicalIndex` applies the filter to postings before the top-k (score only
  admissible rows).
- `VectorIndex` (brute-force, IVF-Flat, HNSW) compiles the predicate to a
  per-request mask via `compile_filter` and holds it during search. All three
  ANN variants share the same protocol, so the semantics cannot drift between
  indexes.

Post-filtering survives only as the benchmark baseline.

## Alternatives

- **Post-filter** — measured: recall@10 0.020, 82% of queries returning zero
  usable results. Not a correctness knob.
- **Post-filter with overfetch** — correct, but the fetch multiplier is set by
  the *query's* date and scales as 1/selectivity; at 1.9% that is scanning
  ~0.7% of the corpus to return 10 rows ([benchmark](../03-benchmarks.md#temporal-pushdown)).
  Also silently degrades on ANN: overfetch via HNSW recovered only 0.4 recall
  at k=2,000 in testing.
- **A per-date index** — correct and fast, but 79 dates × 58 MB, and each new
  snapshot rebuilds one. Unnecessary when a ~1 ms per-request mask exists.

## Consequences

**Positive.** Recall@10 at production selectivity: brute-force 1.000,
IVF-Flat 0.949, HNSW 0.484 (HNSW's ceiling is reachability — see
ADR-0012), versus 0.020 post-filtered. Zero-result rate drops from 82% to
~0% for IVF and brute-force. Filtered lexical search gets *faster* than
unfiltered (5.4 ms vs 21.0 ms), since ~98% of postings are skipped.

**Negative.** Fixed cost becomes data-dependent: filtered IVF probes until it
finds k admissible rows rather than exactly `nprobe` cells, so a latency
*guarantee* becomes a latency *distribution* (ADR-0013 bounds the tail). Every
index now needs `compile_filter`, a real API surface increase. Filter
compilation is O(N) per request (~1 ms at 38k) and cannot in general be
amortized — though a small LRU keyed on `as_of` would serve nearly all traffic
from a warm mask (most queries ask about today).
